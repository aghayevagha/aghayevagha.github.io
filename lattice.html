Lattice-Based Cryptography: A Student's Guide

This page contains supplementary materials and a roadmap for students who aim to advance in lattice-based cryptography. Background in cryptography and linear algebra is necessary, especially understanding why constructions are secure require knowledge in provable security.

Introduction

Modern cryptography is built on mathematical hard problems, which are believed to be infeasable to solve with todays supercomputers efficiently. However, Peter Shor has introduced an efficient algorithm which can solve integer factorization problem and discrete-log problem on any groups. Governments and big companies put billions of dollars for building scalable quantum computers, which might result in serious security problems for contemporary systems. This trend further motivates Post-Quantum Cryotography (PQC), the set of cryptographic constructions which are resistant against attacks by both classical and quantum algorithms. One of the prominent candidates for PQC is lattice-based cryptography. Generally, lattices are grid-like structures which span over n dimensional space, and often work over efficient computations over matrices and vectors. The hard problems defined over lattices have attractive worst-case security guarantees, meaning that solving some average-case hard lattice problems is as hard as solving related hard problems in their worst case.
